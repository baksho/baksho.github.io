### Research motivation

Predicting cancer drug response is an important problem in precision oncology. Predictive performance alone is not enough when models are meant to support a scientific understanding of drug sensitivity and resistance. This project investigates an interpretable deep-learning pipeline that integrates biological knowledge with molecular representations of drugs.

### Objectives

- Develop a deep-learning pipeline for cancer drug-response prediction.
- Integrate Gene Ontology-guided representations of biological processes.
- Incorporate molecular graph-based drug encoders.
- Investigate pathway-level interpretability in multimodal prediction.

### Approach

The pipeline combines Gene Ontology-guided neural networks with molecular graph-based drug encoders. It uses drug-aware gene gating and multimodal fusion to integrate biological and molecular information. Synthetic-data experiments and a GDSC2 data pipeline were implemented to support the investigation.

### Contribution and outcome

The project brings biological prior knowledge, molecular representations, and interpretable neural network components together in one prediction pipeline. It reflects my interest in machine learning approaches that connect predictive modeling with biologically meaningful representations. It produced an interpretable deep-learning pipeline for cancer drug-response prediction, including synthetic-data experiments and a GDSC2 data pipeline.
