Collection 01

Trustworthy ML

Calibration, uncertainty quantification, robustness, and interpretability.

ICML 2017 · DOI ↗

Takeaway

Calibration is distinct from classification accuracy. Temperature scaling offers a simple, effective way to improve the reliability of predicted probabilities without changing the predicted class, but its success must be evaluated in the intended deployment setting.

One-line interpretation

A neural network can be accurate yet overconfident, and a simple one-parameter transformation can often make its confidence estimates substantially more trustworthy.

Collection 02

AI for Science (AI4S)

Methods that help us discover, understand, and investigate the natural world.

Nature 2023 · DOI ↗

Takeaway

AI's greatest potential lies not simply in predicting outcomes, but in helping scientists generate, evaluate, and test hypotheses more efficiently. Realizing that potential requires reliable methods, scientific knowledge, rigorous validation, and collaboration across disciplines.

One-line interpretation

This paper provides a conceptual map of how AI can transform scientific discovery, while explaining why predictive performance alone is not enough to establish scientific reliability or understanding.

Authors: Chuan Guo, Geoff Pleiss, Yu Sun, and Kilian Q. Weinberger.
Venue: Proceedings of the 34th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2017, PMLR 70
DOI: 10.5555/3305381.3305518
Paper type: Empirical research paper with methodological analysis

Problem: Modern neural networks can achieve high classification accuracy while producing unreliable confidence estimates. A model predicting 90% confidence should be correct approximately 90% of the time across predictions at that confidence level, but modern networks often violate this expectation.

Core idea: The authors investigate the factors affecting neural-network calibration and compare several post-hoc calibration methods. Their key contribution is demonstrating that temperature scaling, which divides a model's logits by a single learned scalar before applying softmax, can substantially improve calibration with minimal computational overhead.

Main results: On CIFAR-100, ResNet-110's Expected Calibration Error (ECE) decreases from 16.53% to 1.26% after temperature scaling, using 15 confidence bins. On ImageNet with DenseNet-161, ECE falls from 6.28% to 1.99%. Temperature scaling performs competitively with more complex calibration methods across several evaluated datasets, although its effectiveness varies by dataset.

Key limitations: The experiments assume that training, validation, and test data come from the same distribution. Calibration performance depends on the evaluation metric and dataset, and temperature scaling does not guarantee reliability under distribution shift or provide a complete characterization of predictive uncertainty.

Takeaway: Calibration is distinct from classification accuracy. Temperature scaling offers a simple, effective way to improve the reliability of predicted probabilities without changing the predicted class, but its success must be evaluated in the intended deployment setting.

One-line interpretation: A neural network can be accurate yet overconfident, and a simple one-parameter transformation can often make its confidence estimates substantially more trustworthy.

Authors: Hanchen Wang, Tianfan Fu, Yuanqi Du, Marinka Zitnik, and colleagues
Venue: Nature, 2023, 620, 47–60
DOI: 10.1038/s41586-023-06221-2
Paper type: Review / Survey

Problem: Scientific discovery increasingly requires analyzing massive, complex datasets and exploring enormous spaces of possible hypotheses. Traditional approaches can be limited by experimental costs, insufficient labelled data, and the computational difficulty of identifying promising candidates.

Core idea: AI can accelerate the entire scientific process, starting from data collection and representation learning to hypothesis generation, experiment design, and actual experiment simulation. The paper reviews key approaches, including self-supervised learning, geometric deep learning, transformers, generative models, reinforcement learning, and neural operators.

Main findings: The authors review and discuss existing well-known studies demonstrating AI's potential in a range of scientific domains. They provide several examples including AlphaFold2 for protein structure prediction, AI-guided screening of millions of materials candidates, reinforcement learning for tokamak plasma control, and neural models that accelerate gravitational-wave parameter estimation. This review paper doesn’t conduct any experiments as its own, but share the findings from these cited studies.

Key limitations: Major challenges remain in data quality, generalization under distribution shift, interpretability, uncertainty estimation, reproducibility, computational costs, and responsible deployment. AI-generated predictions and hypotheses still require appropriate scientific validation.

Takeaway: AI's greatest potential lies not simply in predicting outcomes, but in helping scientists generate, evaluate, and test hypotheses more efficiently. Realizing that potential requires reliable methods, scientific knowledge, rigorous validation, and collaboration across disciplines.

One-line interpretation: This paper provides a conceptual map of how AI can transform scientific discovery, while explaining why predictive performance alone is not enough to establish scientific reliability or understanding.
